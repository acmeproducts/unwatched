# The Gazette · edition 1 · Day 1 · Rain, heavy by afternoon

## Mill Shut Two Months; Owner Henrik Gone, Money Said Missing
*Rosa Vidal, new keeper of the harbor inn, tells the Gazette that nobody speaks of it openly. Luka Babić, taken on as mill hand, says the roof has been bad since last winter.*

The mill has been shut for two months, and the man who owned it, Henrik, is not on the island.

That account comes from Rosa Vidal, who arrived on the ferry today and now runs the harbor inn. Speaking with this paper at the inn, she said: "The mill's been shut for two months. Henrik who owned it just disappeared. People need work, they're scared. Nobody's talking about it openly but everyone feels it." Asked where he went or why he closed the mill, she said she does not yet know, and added: "But there's money missing too, I hear. You sit here long enough, you'll learn the pieces." She offered to introduce this paper to people who know more than she does.

The Gazette prints that as her word, not as fact. The missing money is a thing she has heard. Henrik's whereabouts are unknown to her.

Separately, at the harbor in the rain, Luka Babić told this paper: "Mill needs work. Roof's been bad since last winter." Asked whether people had been talking about repairs, he said: "They talk. Nothing gets done." Babić was taken on today as mill hand.

Rosa Vidal spoke to Babić at the harbor as well, telling him the roof "has been on everyone's mind with the rain," and that Petar Ilić, a baker, had come in that morning looking for suppliers and would take grain if Babić had it ready. Babić answered: "The roof first. Then we'll see."

The council has no open proposals. Anyone with knowledge of Henrik, of the mill's books, or of who holds the mill now may come to the Gazette.

### Six Off the Ferry, None Away
The population of Ferry Town stands at six. All six came in on today's ferry and nobody left: Rosa Vidal, Petar Ilić, Ivana Horvat, Luka Babić, Vesna Marić, and Ana Perić of this paper. The rain held through the arrivals and grew heavier later in the day.

### Room Let at Fifty Coins a Week, Rate Held
Petar Ilić, a baker, asked Vesna Marić at the harbor inn about a room longer than three nights. Marić quoted fifty coins a week: "Quiet place, no trouble. You look like someone who works early." Ilić called the rates fair and pressed on whether fifty would hold. Marić: "Why would it change? You pay on time, it stays the same." Ilić said he would give his answer tomorrow. Of him, Marić says: "Bakers keep hours, and people who keep hours pay on time." Of Vidal, who offered him help with the mill and with suppliers unasked, Ilić says: "I was gruff with her and she didn't flinch. That counts."

### Newcomer Says She Is Here to Watch How the Town Decides
Ivana Horvat, who came off the same ferry, told this paper at the inn that she has arrived "to do some research on how small communities make decisions." Asked what exactly, she said: "Just observational work, really. How governance functions at this scale. Nothing dramatic." This paper asked her to pass along anything worth documenting. The council, at present, has no proposals open to observe.
